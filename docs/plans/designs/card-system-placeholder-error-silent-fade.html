Failure the room never sees

Our recommendation: the audience should not be shown an error at all. A failure is meaningful to the presenter and meaningless to the room — an “error” burned into someone’s face on a sales call converts a silent non-event into a visible product defect. The placeholder simply retreats: opacity to zero over 420 ms with a slight scale-down, on the same spring family it entered with. The presenter learns about it privately in the HUD.

Sequence
4 sampled frames of a single 420 ms exit. Nothing new is drawn — the existing placeholder just leaves.
Motion
Reverse of the entry spring (120/20): opacity 1 → 0 with scale 1 → 0.98 and a small outward drift. Reduced motion: a flat 150 ms opacity fade, no scale, no drift.
Why silent
The room has no action to take and no context for the failure. The presenter, who does, is told in the HUD error state — a surface the audience cannot see.
Cost of the alternative
A visible error pill turns a 3-second non-event into a permanent artifact of the recording.
Trade-off
If the presenter said “let me pull that up”, the room briefly sees a placeholder that vanishes. That is a smaller cost than a red failure notice, and the presenter can simply retry.
